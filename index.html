The elevator pitch

What this actually is

Curious

Think of it as three things stitched into one page: a live scoreboard that updates itself, a fortune teller that rates each team's chances before kickoff, and a filing cabinet that keeps your bracket, your calendar, and the weather at every stadium in order — all refreshing quietly while you watch.

Engineer

A full-stack interactive dashboard and prediction suite for the World Cup 2026, built on React 19 + Vite 6 + TypeScript 5.8 with Tailwind v4 and Framer Motion 12 on the front end, served by an Express 4 backend that talks to Firebase Firestore (Admin + Client SDK) and pushes live updates to the browser over Server-Sent Events. It aggregates four external data sources into one interface: fixtures, live scores, weather, and news.

Under the hood

System architecture

Your browser talks to one helper — the server. That helper fetches fresh football facts from around the internet, remembers your settings in a filing cabinet, and hands everything back to your screen.

The React SPA talks exclusively to the Express server, never directly to Firestore or any third-party API. The server is the single choke point: it proxies four external data sources, reads/writes Firestore for persisted settings, and streams live state back over SSE.

The dotted lines show information moving — bright ones are the main path from your screen to the server; dim ones are the server quietly checking the outside world. worldcup26.ir includes a GitHub-mirror fallback for resiliency; all four external calls are proxied server-side so no API keys ever reach the client.

01 / LIVE MATCH CENTER

Every match, updating itself

A countdown badge quietly changes as kickoff gets closer — from "soon" to "today" to "live" — and the score updates on its own, no refreshing needed. Tap a match and you get the full picture: stats, who's favoured to win, and what the weather's like at the stadium.

Match cards subscribe to live score sync and render a countdown state machine (SOON → TODAY → LIVE → FT) driven off kickoff timestamps. Cards use a 3D-tilt hover interaction; tapping opens a modal combining live stats, the win-probability model, and stadium weather.

02 / KNOCKOUT BRACKET

A bracket that fills itself in

The full path from the Round of 32 all the way to the Final — plus the third-place game — laid out as one bracket. Slots that say things like "Runner-up, Group A" quietly turn into real team names the moment that result is known. One click turns the whole thing into an image you can save or share.

Renders the complete 48-team knockout tree (Round of 32 → Final + third-place playoff) with automatic placeholder resolution — slots like W Match 74 or Runner-up Group A resolve as upstream results land. Exports to PNG via html-to-image/html2canvas.

KnockoutBracket
03 / ANALYTICS & PREDICTIONS

A rating for every team, an odds line for every match

Every team gets a tier — from "Elite" down to "Underdog" — based on how strong they look on paper. Before any match, the dashboard turns that into odds: how likely is a home win, a draw, or an away win? You can also put two teams side by side and see the charts.

A Team Strength Index (TSI) tiers every team into ELITE / STRONG / COMPETITIVE / UNDERDOG. A win-probability model derives Home/Draw/Away odds per fixture from those ratings. A head-to-head comparison tool and Recharts visualizations surface group-stage form.

05 / NOTIFICATIONS & CALENDAR

It taps you on the shoulder

Goals, cards, kickoffs, half-time, full-time — the moment they happen, a notification appears in your browser. It'll also nudge you 15 minutes before kickoff, and you can drop any match straight into your calendar or export the whole schedule as a PDF.

An SSE pipeline polls ESPN and pushes goal/card/kickoff/half-time/full-time events to connected browsers instantly, plus browser notifications 15 minutes pre-kickoff. Supports per-match .ics export and a custom PDF schedule generator.

Step by step

The real-time pipeline

Think of this like current flowing through a circuit: something happens on the pitch, and five quiet steps later it's a notification on your screen — all in a second or two.

A one-way event pipeline: ESPN is polled server-side, matched events are emitted through Node's EventEmitter, broadcast over an SSE stream, and rendered client-side as a toast. Watch it loop below.
